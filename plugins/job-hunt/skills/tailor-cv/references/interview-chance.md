# Rating the chance of an interview invite

The number answers one question: if this CV is submitted to this posting, how
likely is a first-interview invite? It's an estimate, not a promise. Say so once.

## Step 1: Match score (0 to 100), from what the tailored CV actually shows

| Area | Weight | What to check |
|---|---|---|
| Core requirements | 40 | Each "required" item: clearly met, partly met, or missing |
| Tech stack / tools | 20 | Share of the named stack that appears in the CV |
| Seniority and years | 15 | Years and level vs. what is asked; too junior or far too senior both cost |
| Domain fit | 15 | Same or adjacent industry or product type |
| Nice-to-haves | 10 | Each nice-to-have the CV shows |

## Step 2: Adjust for competition and practical filters

- **Competition:** well-known company, high pay, or remote and open globally:
  multiply by 0.5 to 0.7. Smaller or less-known company, or location-restricted:
  0.8 to 1.0.
- **Hard filters:** a requirement the CV clearly lacks (work authorisation, a
  required degree with no "or equivalent experience", a required certification
  or must-have technology): cap at 10%.
- **Time zone:** remote but with little working-hours overlap with the user's
  time zone: multiply by about 0.8.
- **Relocation needing sponsorship:** multiply by about 0.8.
- **Hires from the user's country:** evidence they do: multiply by 1.1; no
  evidence for a worldwide-remote role: 0.8.
- **Referral:** if the user has one, add 10 to 20 points.

Round to the nearest 5%. Strong-but-not-perfect matches at competitive
companies usually land between 15% and 35%. Above 50% should be rare.

## Step 3: Explain it

The number, then 2-3 reasons it isn't lower, 2-3 reasons it isn't higher, and
what would raise it, with a rough new figure.
